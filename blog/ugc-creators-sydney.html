How to find UGC creators in Sydney (without an agency)

You do not need an agency retainer to find good creators. You need to know where to look, what to check before you pay anyone, and how to brief them.

LocalCreators team · 27 November 2026 · 5 min read

First, be clear about what UGC means here

UGC stands for user-generated content: photos and videos made by real people about your business, rather than by a studio or an ad agency. A UGC creator is usually a nano creator — someone with 1,000 to 10,000 followers — who visits your shop, tries the product or experience, and posts about it in their own style.

Where to find UGC creators in Sydney

Start where creators already post. Instagram and TikTok search are free: search for your suburb plus your niche — a café in Newtown might look for Newtown food creators, a salon in Parramatta for Parramatta beauty creators. Look at who posts about places like yours, not just who has the most followers.

Creator platforms are the other option. How to vet a creator before you pay them

Follower count is the weakest signal. Check these five things instead:

  • Engagement, not followers. Nano creators have a median engagement rate of 2.1%, versus 0.5% for mega creators, according to a Virev 2026 study of 391,731 Instagram creators. If a creator with 5,000 followers gets only a handful of likes and no comments, something is off.
  • Local audience. Read the comments and look at who follows them. Are the commenters from Sydney? Do they mention your suburb or nearby ones? A creator whose audience is mostly overseas will not fill your tables.
  • Relevant past content. Have they posted about a café, salon, or shop like yours? Past work is the best preview of what you will get.
  • Consistency. Do they post regularly, or once every few months? A creator who posts often is more likely to deliver on time.
  • Disclosure habits. Do their past paid posts carry a clear #ad or Paid Partnership tag? If not, that is a warning sign — for them and for you.

What to pay a Sydney nano creator

Paying fairly matters — for the creator, and for the quality you get back. The Australia Experiences benchmark from August 2026 puts Australian nano creator rates at $100–$400 per post, and micro creator rates at $300–$3,000 per post. A Virev 2026 study of 391,731 Instagram creators found a median sponsored post cost of $110 for a nano creator, versus $636 for a micro creator.

Most small businesses should pay cash plus the product or experience — a free meal for two, or a free treatment — rather than product alone. A small cash payment signals that you value the work, and gives you the right to expect a proper brief, a deadline, and a revision if something is wrong.

How to brief a creator (without killing the content)

A good brief is short: what to show, what to say, and what not to promise. Include the address and the best time to visit, the dish or service to feature, any offer for followers, and the disclosure tag you need. Then let the creator use their own words.

Agree on the basics in writing before they visit: how many posts, which format (a Reel plus a Story works well), when it goes live, and whether you approve it first. On LocalCreators, you approve every post before it goes live, and disclosure tags are added automatically — but if you are hiring directly, build that approval step in yourself. Never skip it.

Disclosure: the part you cannot skip

Paid creator posts in Australia must be clearly disclosed. In March 2026 the ACCC fined PhotobookShop $39,600 for influencer disclosure failures. Every paid post needs a clear #ad or Paid Partnership label, visible without tapping "more". Tell the creator upfront, check the post before it publishes, and do not accept one that hides the disclosure.

When to stop doing it yourself

Finding one creator yourself is manageable. Finding ten, briefing them all, chasing drafts, checking disclosure, and tracking results is a part-time job.
